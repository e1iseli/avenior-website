After three years of thinking, her art brand finally began.

— total views

In Dubai, Fay had a stable career—and an idea she had carried for three years: bring the work of emerging young Chinese artists to a new audience.

After graduating from UCL, Fay moved to Dubai and joined one of the world’s top ten investment and construction groups as a Commercial Engineer. Her education was in architecture and the built environment. She had never worked in technology and had no experience building a website with AI.

Outside her professional life, however, one idea kept returning. She wanted to create an art brand that introduced work by young Chinese artists to private buyers and to the owners of cafés, shops and other commercial spaces in Dubai.

She first began thinking about it in 2023, while still living in the UK. The idea followed her to Dubai, but it never became real—not because she lacked commitment, but because she could not see the first practical step.

01 / A three-year ideaThe opportunity had been forming for years.

Fay enjoys exhibitions and regularly buys cultural and creative products. In 2023, she and a friend had already discussed trying the idea together. Each year, she followed graduate exhibitions at different art schools, met emerging artists, developed potential supply relationships and researched eBay and specialist art marketplaces.

After moving to Dubai, she began observing the local cultural-products market more closely. In a city with such a large international population, could there be customers interested in work by young Chinese artists?

She could see an opening—and she had access to resources others might not. Fay knew young artists in China: some were still studying, while others had graduated and established their own studios. They had work, ideas and a desire to find buyers.

On one side was a growing network of artists. On the other was a Dubai market she wanted to understand. Slowly, the outline of a business began to appear.

02 / Owning the platformWhen existing marketplaces did not fit, she decided to build a brand.

Fay had access to artists but lacked a platform she could truly shape. Some specialist art marketplaces expect artists to have established exhibition histories, major awards or recognised credentials. Yet the people she most wanted to support were precisely those who had not yet become widely known.

They might not have appeared in major exhibitions or won prestigious awards, but their work still deserved to be seen.

For Fay, an independent art website meant agency. She could decide which artists to feature, how to organise and present their work, and what the brand should feel like. She would not be limited by a third party’s entry rules or presentation system.

She could create a visual language, tell the story behind each artist and help the right work meet genuinely interested buyers. The question was simple and intimidating: how does someone without a technical background build that from zero?

03 / The Avenior courseThe mentor did not begin with tools. She began with the idea.

Fay joined Avenior’s AI Website Design & Development course. What surprised her was that the mentor did not begin with software or technical instructions. The first conversation was about the business: the people Fay wanted to serve, the value she hoped to create and what success might look like.

A commercial website is more than a collection of pages and images. It must answer deeper questions. Who is the brand for? What does it stand for? Which visual direction fits the idea? How should the work be presented so potential customers understand both the art and the proposition?

For Fay’s project, positioning, artwork presentation and the overall visual experience would all shape the kind of buyers she hoped to attract.

Once the direction was clearer, the mentor offered brand and design guidance and taught Fay how to use AI to build a commercial website. One-to-one critiques allowed her to receive feedback on her own project, rather than simply following a generic tutorial.

She was not only learning an AI website tool. She was learning how to plan a brand and a digital experience around a real business idea.

Her biggest outcome was not a feature or a tool. It was the feeling that everything finally had a plan.

04 / Confidence to beginThe idea stopped feeling abstract.

Before the course, Fay’s plan to sell art through a website felt vague. She did not know where to start. Afterwards, she felt genuine confidence that she could create a working site.

Many people recognise this pattern. You have a side-business idea, notice a market opportunity or already possess useful relationships. You may want to build a personal brand, develop a small product or turn professional knowledge into a service.

You research it for months or years, yet every attempt to begin creates a new question. What about the website? The design? The technology? What would it cost to hire someone? The project remains in your mind.

Fay’s experience shows that having an idea and knowing how to begin are different things. When someone helps you clarify the business direction, guides the brand and design choices, and teaches you how to use AI to translate the idea into a website, complexity becomes a sequence of manageable actions.

AI cannot find customers automatically or guarantee that a side business will succeed. It can, however, become a tool for action. Mentoring, project-specific advice and one-to-one feedback reduce the amount of blind experimentation required.

05 / Creating in the AI eraYou do not need to become a technical expert before you start.

Fay’s story matters because her most valuable inputs were not technical. She had an education in architecture and the built environment, professional experience in commercial engineering, a network of young artists and a point of view about an opportunity in Dubai.

AI did not decide what business she should create, nor could it validate the demand in Dubai on her behalf. It offered a different possibility: someone with expertise, relationships and creative ambition no longer had to abandon an idea simply because she lacked a conventional technology background.

You do not need to master every technical skill before you have permission to build. You can begin with the idea, clarify the brand and design direction with a mentor, learn to create a commercial website with AI and improve it through feedback from real customers.

This is why Avenior created the AI Website Design & Development course. We want AI to become a practical creative tool for people from many different backgrounds—not something reserved for a small group of technical specialists.

Sometimes the thing that changes a project is not a magical tool. It is the first moment you believe: I can begin this.

Your idea deserves a first step.
